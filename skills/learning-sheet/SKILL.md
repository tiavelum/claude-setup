# Make a learning sheet

## Settle the purpose

1. Ask what the sheet is for when the request leaves it open: to understand and memorise a topic, or to look things up. This skill makes the first; for the second, make a reference document instead.
2. Ask whether the person already pictures the topic in some way, and start from that picture.
3. Write the sheet for the topic in general, and name products or services only as examples, unless the person asks for one particular service.

## Find the scene

1. Choose one scene from everyday life in which every item of the topic has its place, such as a house with a key cabinet and buildings in town for logins and their recovery.
2. Give every item one sentence in that scene, and one icon that stands for it wherever it appears.
3. Sort the items into two to four zones of the scene, such as at home, in town and if lost. Give each zone one colour and one marker shape that both pages repeat, and a key on page one.

## Lay out the pages

1. Make the sheet as a Design artifact with one artboard per page at A4, 794 × 1123 px, or at Letter for a North American reader, so that each artboard prints as one page. Where no Design type is offered, make a document with the same two parts.
2. Page one holds the pictures: the scene as one illustration with numbered markers that a legend above it names, an illustrated pair for the one contrast that matters most, and a card for each remaining item with its icon, name and sentence.
3. Page two holds the details: a table with one row per item, giving its icon and name, what it is, and the two or three properties a reader compares, such as whether it is enough on its own and where to keep it, grouped by the zones of page one. Below the table, give the few questions and rules to remember.
4. Set all text at 12 px (9 pt) or larger, dark on a light ground, so that it reads when printed in grayscale.
5. Budget each page's height before writing it, since anything past the edge of a fixed page is cut off.

## Hand it over

Where you cannot look at the rendered pages yourself, ask the person to check both pages for text that is cut off or overlaps.

## Improve this skill

At the end of each session that makes a learning sheet:

1. List what the person corrected or asked to change while the sheet was made.
2. For each correction that a step of this skill would have avoided, propose that step with its exact text, and ask whether to add it.
