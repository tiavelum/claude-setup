# Make a learning sheet

## Settle the purpose

1. Ask what the sheet is for when the request leaves it open: to understand and memorise a topic, or to look things up. This skill makes the first; for the second, make a reference document instead.
2. Ask whether the person already pictures the topic in some way, and start from that picture.
3. Write the sheet for the topic in general, and name products or services only as examples, unless the person asks for one particular service.

## Find the scene

1. Choose one scene from everyday life in which every item of the topic has its place, such as a house with a key cabinet and buildings in town for logins and their recovery.
2. Give every item one name, one sentence in that scene and one icon, and use the same three wherever the item appears.
3. Sort the items into two to four zones of the scene, such as at home, in town and if lost. Give each zone one colour and one marker shape that both pages repeat, and a key on page one.
4. Before you lay out the pages, have an agent that did not see the scene being made check it as text: give it the topic, the scene, the zones, and each item with its name, sentence and zone. Have it read them as a learner who does not know the topic, check each sentence against public sources, and report each item whose place in the scene is unclear, each part that contradicts itself or the topic, such as a key kept in the cabinet it opens or a service said to keep a copy of a password, and each question the learner would still ask.
5. Apply each finding, or put it to the person with your reason for leaving it, before you go on.

## Lay out the pages

1. Make the sheet as a Design artifact with one artboard per page at A4, 794 × 1123 px, or at Letter for a North American reader, so that each artboard prints as one page. Where no Design type is offered, make a document with the same two parts.
2. Page one holds the pictures: the scene as one illustration with numbered markers that a legend above it names, an illustrated pair for the one contrast that matters most, and a card for each remaining item with its icon, name and sentence.
3. Page two holds the details: a table with one row per item of page one, under the same name, giving its icon and name, what it is, and the two or three properties a reader compares, such as whether it is enough on its own and where to keep it, grouped by the zones of page one. Below the table, give the few questions and rules to remember.
4. Set all text at 12 px (9 pt) or larger, dark on a light ground, so that it reads when printed in grayscale.
5. Budget each page's height before writing it, with at least 28 px (7 mm) left at every edge, since anything past the edge of a fixed page is cut off and many printers leave the outermost millimetres blank.

## Check the pages

1. Render each page to an image at its print size, with the sheet's own fonts, since another font moves the line breaks. Where the font service is blocked, take the fonts from a package registry.
2. Have an agent that did not see the sheet being made check the images and the pages' text for print: have it measure each page's content height against the page height, and report text that is cut off, overlaps or is smaller than 12 px, a legend number without its marker, a zone whose colour or marker shape changes from one place to another, and a row of page two whose name or zone differs from page one. Leave the meaning to the check of the scene.
3. Apply each finding, or put it to the person with your reason for leaving it; then render again and look at what changed.

## Hand it over

Where you could not render the pages, ask the person to check both pages for text that is cut off or overlaps. Where no agent could check the scene or the pages, tell the person that no second reader checked them.

## Improve this skill

At the end of each session that makes a learning sheet:

1. List what the person corrected or asked to change while the sheet was made.
2. For each correction that a step of this skill would have avoided, propose that step with its exact text, and ask whether to add it.
