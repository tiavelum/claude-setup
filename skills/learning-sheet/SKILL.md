# Make a learning sheet

## Settle the purpose

1. Ask what the sheet is for when the request leaves it open: to understand and memorise a topic, or to look things up. This skill makes the first; for the second, make a reference document instead.
2. Ask whether the person already pictures the topic in some way, and start from that picture.
3. Write the sheet for the topic in general, and name products or services only as examples, unless the person asks for one particular service.

## Find the scene

1. Choose one scene from everyday life in which every item of the topic has its place, or for a process its step along one route, such as a house with a key cabinet and buildings in town for logins and their recovery.
2. Give every item one name, the term the learner will meet elsewhere, one sentence that places it in the scene and gives it a job no other item has, and one icon, and use the same three wherever the item appears.
3. Sort the items into two to four zones by one rule used for all zones: the places of the scene, such as at home, in town and if lost, the phases of a process, or the groups of roles. Make each item belong to exactly one zone. Where the topic is a sequence, number the items in the order they happen and make each zone a run of that order. Give each zone one colour and one marker shape that both pages repeat, and a key to the zones on page one.
4. Before you lay out the pages, have an agent that did not see the scene being made check it as text: give it the topic, the scene, the zones, and each item with its name, sentence, zone and a one-line description of its icon. Have it read them as a learner who does not know the topic, check each sentence against public sources, and report each item whose place in the scene is unclear, each part that contradicts itself or the topic, such as an item that needs itself to work or an icon that gives an item a property the real one lacks, and each question the learner would still ask.
5. Apply each finding, or put it to the person with your reason for leaving it, before you go on.

## Lay out the pages

1. Make the sheet as a Design artifact with one artboard per page at A4, 794 × 1123 px, or at Letter, 816 × 1056 px, for a North American reader, so that each artboard prints as one page. Where no Design type is offered, make a document with the same two parts.
2. Set all text at 12 px (9 pt) or larger, dark on a light ground, so that it reads when printed in grayscale.
3. Budget each page's height before writing it, with at least 28 px (about 7 mm) left at every edge, since [many printers leave the outermost millimetres blank](https://support.microsoft.com/en-US/publisher/set-page-margins-for-printing-using-publisher).
4. Page one holds the pictures: the scene as one illustration with a numbered marker for each item it draws and a legend above it that names them, a card with icon, name and sentence for each item the scene does not draw, and, where the topic has one contrast that matters most, an illustrated pair for it. Where the items are numbered, show each item's number on its marker or card.
5. Page two holds the details: a table with one row per item of page one, under the same name, giving its icon and name, what it is, and the two or three properties on which the items differ, such as what it does, what it needs and what happens without it, grouped by the zones of page one. Below the table, give the few questions and rules to remember.

## Check the pages

1. Render each page to an image at its print size, with the sheet's own fonts, since another font moves the line breaks. Where the font service is blocked, take the fonts from a package registry.
2. Have an agent that did not see the sheet being made check the pages for print: give it the page images, the page source, the page size, the 28 px edge minimum and the 12 px text minimum. Have it measure each page's content height against the page height and report, with page and place, text that is cut off, overlaps or is smaller than 12 px, content within 28 px of an edge, a legend number without its marker, a zone whose colour or marker shape changes from one place to another, an item whose icon differs between the pages, and a row of page two whose name or zone differs from page one. Leave the meaning to the check of the scene.
3. Apply each finding, or put it to the person with your reason for leaving it; then render again and look at what changed.

## Hand it over

1. Hand over both pages in a form that prints one page per sheet, and name the paper size they are made for.
2. Where you could not render the pages, ask the person to check both pages for text that is cut off or overlaps.
3. Where no agent could check the scene or the pages, tell the person that no second reader checked them.

## Improve this skill

At the end of each session that makes a learning sheet:

1. List what the person corrected or asked to change while the sheet was made.
2. For each correction that a step of this skill would have avoided, propose that step with its exact text, and ask whether to add it.
