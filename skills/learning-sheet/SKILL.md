# Make a learning sheet

## Settle the purpose

1. Look for a sheet on the topic where the person's instructions say learning sheets are kept, as that repository's README says. Where the instructions name no such place, or no sheet there is on the topic, go on with the next step; where you cannot look there, tell the person that you could not, and go on.
2. Where a sheet on the topic is there, ask the person whether to build on it as a new version of that sheet or to make a variant beside it with a name of its own; where several are there, such as variants, name them and ask which one. Where they build on it, start from that sheet and its scene, as that repository's README says for changing a sheet, and skip the rest of Settle the purpose and Find the scene except for what the person wants changed.
3. Ask what the sheet is for when the request leaves it open: to understand and memorise a topic, or to look things up. This skill makes the first; for the second, make a reference document instead.
4. Ask whether the person already pictures the topic in some way, and start from that picture.
5. Write the sheet for the topic in general, and name products or services only as examples, unless the person asks for one particular service.

## Find the scene

1. Sketch two or three candidate scenes from everyday life of different kinds, such as a place where things are kept for items someone holds, a journey or errand for the steps of a process, a team or a kitchen for roles that work together, or a landscape for directions and forces; where the person already pictures the topic, make that picture the first candidate. Choose the one whose structure matches the topic's, in which every item has its place, its role or, for a process, its step along one route.
2. Give every item one name, the term the learner will meet elsewhere, one sentence that places it in the scene and gives it a job no other item has, and one icon, and use the same three wherever the item appears.
3. Sort the items into two to four zones by one rule used for all zones: the places of the scene, the phases of a process, or the groups of roles. Make each item belong to exactly one zone. Where the topic is a sequence, order the items as they happen and make each zone a run of that order. Give each zone one colour and one marker shape that both pages repeat, and a key to the zones on page one.
4. Within a zone, keep items that are alternatives to each other together, and show an item that adds to another beside the item it adds to, never among the alternatives; give it the zone of the item it adds to. Then give each item one number, counting zone by zone in the order of the zones and within a zone from the first group to the last, or as the items happen where the topic is a sequence, and use it wherever the item appears.
5. Before you lay out the pages, have an agent that did not see the scene being made check it as text: give it the topic, the scene, the zones, and each item with its number, name, sentence, zone and a one-line description of its icon. Have it read them as a learner who does not know the topic, check each sentence against public sources, and report each item whose place, role or step in the scene is unclear, each part that contradicts itself or the topic, such as an item that needs itself to work or an icon that gives an item a property the real one lacks, and each question the learner would still ask.
6. Apply each finding, or put it to the person with your reason for leaving it, before you go on.

## Lay out the pages

1. Make the sheet as a Design artifact with one artboard per page at A4, 794 × 1123 px, or at Letter, 816 × 1056 px, for a North American reader, so that each artboard prints as one page. Where no Design type is offered, make a document with the same two parts.
2. Set all text at 12 px (9 pt) or larger, dark on a light ground, so that it reads when printed in grayscale.
3. Budget each page's height before writing it, with at least 40 px (about 11 mm) left at every edge, since [many printers leave the outermost millimetres blank](https://support.microsoft.com/en-US/publisher/set-page-margins-for-printing-using-publisher), and a further 40 px spare at the bottom, since another browser may break lines differently from your render.
4. Give every column of a table and every card a width in px, and set the table's layout to fixed, so that no other browser can redistribute the space.
5. Page one tells the scene. Open it with a brief of at most two lines that says, in plain words, that the scene is a picture of the topic and that page two gives the facts behind each numbered item. Then give it a scene section, followed by one section for each group that Find the scene step 4 keeps together (for a sequence, each zone) that has an item the scene does not draw. The scene section holds the scene as one illustration with a numbered marker for each item it draws and a legend above it that names them. A group section holds a card with icon, number, name and sentence for each of its items that the scene does not draw. Where the topic has one contrast that matters most, show the group that holds it as one illustrated contrast with all its items' numbers, instead of cards. Head each group section with its zone, and tint every card, and the background of the illustrated contrast, with a pale shade of its zone's colour. Write page one's sentences in the scene's words only, naming each item by its term and leaving every technical fact to page two.
6. Page two gives the facts, in plain technical language: one table with one header row and one row per item of page one, under the same number, name and icon, giving what it is and the two or three properties on which the items differ, such as what it does, what it needs and what happens without it. Make one row group for the items of page one's scene section, tinted a neutral pale grey since it mixes zones, and one row group for each group section of page one, in page one's order, with the rows of each row group in number order. Start each row group with one title row across all columns, never a table inside the table. Tint a row group like its section's cards, put the section's zone marker in its title, and name no item in a title. Show each row's zone marker beside its number. Below the table, give the few questions and rules to remember.

## Check the pages

1. Render each page to an image at its print size, with the sheet's own fonts, since another font moves the line breaks. Where the font service is blocked, take the fonts from a package registry.
2. Have an agent that did not see the sheet being made check the pages for print: give it the page images, the page source, the page size, the 40 px edge minimum and the 12 px text minimum. Have it measure each page's content height against the page height, also with 0.3 px more letter-spacing on all body text, and report, with page and place, text that is cut off, overlaps or is smaller than 12 px, content within 40 px of an edge, content that the extra letter-spacing pushes past the bottom margin, a legend number without its marker, a zone whose colour or marker shape changes from one place to another, an item whose icon differs between the pages, an item without its number, and a row of page two whose number, name or group differs from page one.
3. Have the same agent check every sentence written after the scene check, such as page two's details, questions and rules, against public sources, and report each one that is wrong, unclear to a learner, or contradicted by another, such as a rule that a row of the table contradicts.
4. Apply each finding, or put it to the person with your reason for leaving it; then render again, look at what changed, and have the same agent check each sentence you rewrote.

## Hand it over

1. Look at both pages as the person's own browser shows them, or as they export, where you can reach either, and fix what is cut off or overlaps.
2. Hand over both pages in a form that prints one page per sheet, and name the paper size they are made for.
3. Where you could not see the pages as the person will, ask them to export both pages and check that nothing is cut off or overlaps.
4. Where no agent could check the scene or the pages, tell the person that no second reader checked them.
5. Archive the sheet where the person's instructions say learning sheets are kept, as that repository's README says, at every hand-over and again after each later change to the sheet, so that the sheet outlives its session and its artifact.
6. Where the person's instructions name no such place, or you cannot follow that repository's README for this sheet, tell the person that the sheet is not archived, and why.

## Improve this skill

At the end of each session that makes a learning sheet:

1. List what the person corrected or asked to change while the sheet was made.
2. For each correction that a step of this skill would have avoided, propose that step with its exact text, and ask whether to add it.
